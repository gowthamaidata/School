# The 20-minute principal demo

A script for the meeting that decides whether you get a pilot. It is built
around five moments where the principal's attitude shifts. Everything else is
supporting material.

**Before you walk in:** open the app on your phone, sign in as Principal, and
leave it on the dashboard. Never open a laptop first — the whole argument is
that this works on a phone, so demonstrate that by using one.

---

## Opening (2 minutes)

Do not pitch. Ask:

> "Sir/Madam, before I show you anything — how does the school currently
> know how many students were absent today?"

Then listen. Whatever they answer (register, phone calls, the office
computer) is the thing you contrast against for the rest of the meeting.

A useful follow-up:

> "And if a parent calls at 11 a.m. asking whether their child reached
> school, who can answer that?"

Write down what they say. You will use their own words later.

---

## WOW #1 — The 90-second school (3 minutes)

**Hand them your phone.** Do not narrate. Let them scroll.

The dashboard shows today's attendance percentage, fee collection against
target, the number of families with dues, and which classes have not marked
attendance yet.

Stay quiet for a good ten seconds. Then:

> "That is the whole school. Attendance, money, and which teachers haven't
> submitted yet. You can see this from home at 7 a.m., before you leave."

**What you're aiming for:** "I've always wanted something like this."

If they linger on the fee number, that is your strongest signal — go to
WOW #5 next instead of following this order.

---

## WOW #2 — The 20-second attendance (4 minutes)

Switch to **Attendance**. Pick a class.

> "Your teachers do this on their own phone, in the classroom, during roll
> call."

Start tapping. **The app times you automatically** — a stopwatch appears on
the first tap and stops when you submit. Do not mention it until you finish.

Submit, then point at the number:

> "Eighteen seconds. Right now that's five minutes of register writing,
> plus someone re-entering it into Excel at the end of the month."

Then the part that actually matters:

> "And the moment I pressed submit, every absent child's parent got a
> WhatsApp message. Nobody made a phone call."

**The contrast is the WOW, not the feature.** Let them do the arithmetic on
40 teachers × 5 minutes × 200 school days themselves. If they don't, do it
out loud once: that is roughly 660 hours of teacher time a year.

---

## WOW #3 — The instant parent message (3 minutes)

Go to **Messages**. Type something real, about their school:

> "Half-Yearly examinations begin on 5 October. The detailed timetable has
> been shared with class teachers."

Send it. Point at the delivery count.

> "512 parents. One message. Instead of your office manager calling them
> one by one, or a WhatsApp group where half the parents have muted it."

Then the line that lands with principals specifically:

> "And you can see who has read it. When a parent says 'I was never
> informed', you'll know."

---

## WOW #4 — The report card (4 minutes)

Go to **Exams & Marks**. Show marks already entered for a class — point out
the class average and pass percentage updating live.

> "The teacher enters marks once. That's the only time anyone types them."

Now open a student's report card. **Press Print.**

Let the print preview sit on screen. A formatted A4 progress report with the
school letterhead, grades, class rank, attendance, and signature blocks.

> "How long does report card day take your office right now?"

The honest answer in most schools is two to three days of staff time with
Excel, Word and a lot of re-typing. Wait for them to say it.

> "This is the whole school, ready to print, the moment the last mark is
> entered."

---

## WOW #5 — The fee recovery (3 minutes)

Go to **Fees**. Filter to pending.

> "₹23 lakh outstanding this term. 177 families."

Tap **Remind all pending families.**

> "Your office admin spends every Friday afternoon chasing these by phone.
> That was ten seconds."

Then show recording a payment and printing the receipt.

> "When a parent pays at the counter, the receipt prints from here, and the
> outstanding number on your dashboard updates immediately."

**Principals worried about cash flow lean forward here.** If they do, stop
selling and start talking about the pilot.

---

## The Tamil moment (1 minute)

Whenever it fits naturally — usually during attendance — tap the language
toggle.

> "Your teachers can use it in Tamil. Their choice, not the school's."

For matriculation-board schools with older teaching staff, this single
detail closes objections about whether teachers will adopt it. No national
competitor offers it.

---

## Closing the pilot (3 minutes)

Do not present a price list. Ask for a small commitment:

> "I'd like to run this with one class — say 8-A — for a month. Free. Your
> class teacher marks attendance on it, parents get the messages, and at the
> end of the month you tell me whether it saved anyone time.
>
> If it didn't, I'll have wasted my own time and you've lost nothing."

This works because it is genuinely low risk for them, and because one class
using it properly generates the parent reaction that sells the rest.

### When they ask the price

Answer plainly, and frame it against numbers they already know:

> "₹200 per student per year. For your 500 students, that's ₹1 lakh —
> about what you collect from four students in fees."

Do not discount in the first meeting. If they push, offer more time free
rather than a lower rate — a lower rate becomes the anchor for every future
school in the district, because principals talk to each other.

### When they ask "does it do X?"

X will be transport, library, payroll, biometrics, or online payment. All are
on the roadmap; none are built. Say so, and turn it into a partnership:

> "Not yet. I build based on what my school partners need most. If you come
> in during the pilot phase, you tell me what to build next — and you get it
> at no extra cost."

Never claim a feature you have not built. One discovered exaggeration costs
you the contract and the referral.

## Questions you will be asked

**"Where is our data stored?"**
On servers in Mumbai. Only your school's staff can see your school's data —
that's enforced by the database itself, not just the app. India's DPDP Act
2023 covers children's data specifically, and this is built for it.

**"What if the internet goes down?"**
Attendance keeps working on the phone and syncs when the connection is back.
Show them: turn on airplane mode and mark a class.

**"Our teachers aren't technical."**
Hand your phone to the least technical person in the room and ask them to
mark one class. They will manage it. That demonstration is worth more than
any reassurance.

**"We already use [Entab / Fedena / a local vendor]."**
Do not attack the incumbent. Ask: "What does your staff complain about most
with it?" Then show that one thing working better. Switching cost is real, so
you need to be obviously better at the thing that annoys them daily, not
marginally better overall.

**"Can we get it in our school's name and colours?"**
Yes — that's a `docs/CUSTOMISE.md` job and takes about an hour. Offer to have
it branded before the pilot starts. It makes the pilot feel real.

---

## After the meeting

Same day, send one WhatsApp message with:

- The demo link, so they can show it to the correspondent
- One line on what the pilot involves
- A specific next date: *"Shall I set up 8-A next Monday?"*

A vague "let me know" ends most of these conversations. A specific date
gets a yes or a no, and both are useful.
